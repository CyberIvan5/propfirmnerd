How to pass a prop firm challenge

Updated October 16, 2026. PropFirmNerd editorial team

Passing is mostly a sizing problem. The profit target is usually larger than the max loss, so the job is to survive long enough for your edge to show, without trading so small that the monthly fee eats you first.

Start from the max loss, not the target

On a 50K account with a $2,000 max loss, a trade that risks $400 means five losses in a row end the account. Losing streaks of five happen regularly even to good traders. Risking $200 gives you ten. Decide how many consecutive losses you want to survive, divide the max loss by that number, and size every trade from the result with a position size calculator.

Set your own daily stop

Stop for the day well before any firm daily limit. A personal stop at a quarter of the max loss means a bad day costs a quarter of your room, not all of it.

Know how your limit moves

With intraday trailing, open profit you give back still tightens your limit, so take partial profits or use smaller targets. With end-of-day trailing, only the close matters. Read how trailing drawdown works and check your firm's rule.

Respect the consistency rule from day one

If your firm has one, a single huge day can hold up your pass or payout. See consistency rules explained.

Test the plan before you pay

Put your win rate and average win and loss into the pass probability calculator. If it says you bust more often than you pass, change the size before you spend money, not after.

Count the full cost

Monthly rebills and resets add up. Use the true cost calculator and set a budget for attempts before you start.

Questions traders ask

What is the pass rate for prop firm challenges?

Firms rarely publish verified pass rates, and figures quoted online are hard to check. What is clear from the business model is that most buyers do not pass. We do not publish pass rates we cannot source.

How long does it take to pass?

It depends on your size and the target. Many firms set no time limit but charge monthly, so slow passes cost more. Model it with the pass probability calculator.
