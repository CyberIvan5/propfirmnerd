Lucid Trading vs Apex Trader Funding

Checked October 16, 2026 against both firms' own pages. Some links earn us a commission. Disclosure

Both sell one-time 50K evaluations with a $3,000 target and a $2,000 max loss. Lucid publishes its price ($136 for LucidFlex), uses end-of-day drawdown on every plan and has no consistency rule on LucidFlex once funded. Apex did not show its current 50K evaluation prices when we checked, has no consistency rule in the evaluation, and lets you choose end-of-day or intraday trailing drawdown.

Which to pick

Pick Lucid if you want a known price, end-of-day drawdown and bots allowed. Pick Apex if you want its payout ladder: the 50K end-of-day account pays $1,500 rising to $3,000 per request, but you need a $52,600 balance to request and the account closes after six payouts. LucidFlex caps each request at $2,000 and moves you to live after 5 payouts.

Rules that trip people up

On an Apex funded account no single day may be 50% or more of the profit since your last payout. Apex bans automation entirely; Lucid allows bots and copiers but not HFT or microscalping. Both make you close before the end of the session: Lucid at 4:45 PM ET, Apex before market close.

Questions traders ask

What is the difference between Lucid Trading and Apex Trader Funding drawdown?

Lucid Trading uses end-of-day trailing drawdown with a 50K max loss of $2,000 on its main plan. Apex Trader Funding uses a drawdown type that depends on the plan with a 50K max loss of $2,000 on its main plan. Figures as of October 16, 2026.

Does Lucid Trading or Apex Trader Funding have a consistency rule?

Lucid Trading applies a consistency rule in the evaluation only. Apex Trader Funding applies a consistency rule on funded accounts only.

Do Lucid Trading and Apex Trader Funding accept US traders?

Yes. Both Lucid Trading and Apex Trader Funding accept US residents, according to their own pages (as of October 16, 2026).
